Rocky Hill and Blue Sea art, wired to their maps

Both sheets came in as photos of crayon drawings on a wooden table, so they go
through the same builder as the meadow: tools/build_bg.py now takes --src,
--out16 and --bias, and produces a rectified, white-balanced 16:9 backdrop.

Bias (how much of the crop comes off the top) was chosen from side-by-side
crops: 0.2 for the hill keeps sun, peak and rocky ground; 0.3 for the sea keeps
sun, horizon and waves. The drawings are 1.27:1 against a 1.78:1 canvas, so a
third of the height has to go somewhere — this is where it went.

Because the ground sits at a different height in each drawing, the spawn band is
now per map (field: in MAPS): 294-430 meadow, 405-545 hill, 410-540 sea. The
depth scale uses the same per-map band.

Grey rock creatures on a grey mountain were unreadable, so each Pokémon gets a
cream glow outline (Phaser FX) with a soft radial halo behind it as the
non-WebGL fallback.

The menu now shows the selected map's backdrop and swaps it when you change the
chips, so you can see where you're going before pressing PLAY.
